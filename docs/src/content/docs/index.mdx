## Eight lines and a port

No database to provision and no build step. Bring your own models, whether that is Ollama on the same
machine or an API key you already have.

[Read the installation guide](/guides/installation/)

</div>

<div class="start__code">

```yaml title="docker-compose.yml"
services:
  llooma:
    image: ghcr.io/cedhuf/llooma:latest
    restart: unless-stopped
    ports:
      - '4173:4173'
    environment:
      - VITE_ALLOWED_HOSTS=localhost
```

<article class="panel">
	<div class="panel__copy">
		<p class="eyebrow">Library</p>
		<h2>Characters, procedures and notes, kept</h2>
		<p>A persona carries its own prompt, model, avatar and voice, and answers a single turn when you mention it with <code>@</code>. A playbook is a procedure written once and switched on where it is needed. Knowledge is the text you were retyping into every conversation.</p>
		<p>All three install from a store over the network, so adding one is a pull request rather than a release.</p>
		<p class="more"><a href="/behaviour/personas/">How personas work</a></p>
	</div>
	<figure class="panel__shot">
		<Image src={library} alt="The library, with three personas" />
	</figure>
</article>

<article class="panel">
	<div class="panel__copy">
		<p class="eyebrow">Conversations</p>
		<h2>Long ones keep fitting</h2>
		<p>When a conversation outgrows the context window, <code>/compact</code> replaces what was said with a structured summary. Nothing is deleted and one click puts it all back.</p>
		<p>The reply is written on the server, so reloading the page, walking away or letting the phone sleep no longer costs you the answer.</p>
		<p class="more"><a href="/chat/compaction/">How compaction works</a></p>
	</div>
	<figure class="panel__shot">
		<Image src={conversation} alt="A conversation, with the composer at the foot" />
	</figure>
</article>

<article class="panel">
	<div class="panel__copy">
		<p class="eyebrow">On a phone</p>
		<h2>A second interface, not a smaller one</h2>
		<p>One column, a tab bar where a thumb already is, and the microphone set apart because talking is what it is for. Installable as a PWA, and offered to phones only.</p>
		<p>Speaking is a loop rather than a button: it stops listening once the room goes quiet, answers out loud, and listens again. It works with the phone face down on a table.</p>
